1. Scope and responsible organisations

This Policy applies to psychcon.org, app.psychcon.org, applicant and member accounts, evidence assessment, correspondence, certificate registers, payments and SaaS. Towarzystwo Doradców Psychologicznych (The Psychology Counselling Association), KRS 0000819107, NIP 6121869763, REGON 385092831, ul. Warszawska 1/3, 59-700 Bolesławiec, Poland, office@psychcon.org, determines the purposes and rules of processing connected with private titles, Committee decisions and Association registers.

Aleksandra Górczyńska-Kołodziej, Australian sole trader, ABN 33 257 573 623, 26 River Street, Mackay QLD 4740, Australia, provides SaaS, platform infrastructure, payment administration and technical operations. For her own services, sales, accounting and security she may be an independent controller. For technical handling of TDP case files she may act as a processor on the Association's documented instructions. The specific roles depend on actual activities and applicable contracts, not simply on labels.

Questions about privacy may be addressed to office@psychcon.org and will be directed to the relevant party.

02

2. Personal data collected and its sources

Depending on the service, we may process names, email addresses and contact details, professional background, educational qualifications, degrees, certificates, work and training histories, publications and DOI references, application forms, supporting files, correspondence, Committee notes, decisions, document revisions and certification status.

We may also process account/session identifiers, authentication events, IP addresses, device and security logs, order and invoice details, payment status, subscription entitlements, communication preferences and consent history. Data comes from individuals, their Platform activity, authorised assessors and transaction providers.

03

3. Purposes and legal grounds

We use personal data to create and secure accounts, manage applications and supporting documents, assess documented achievements, communicate with the Committee, issue and verify certificates, provide SaaS access, take and account for payments, comply with law, handle complaints and prevent fraud.

Where the GDPR applies, grounds may be Article 6(1)(b) (contract or pre-contractual steps), 6(1)(c) (legal duty), 6(1)(f) (legitimate interests such as security, integrity and claims) and 6(1)(a) (consent for optional marketing or technologies). Processing special-category data, if genuinely required, additionally needs a valid Article 9 condition. Consent may be withdrawn prospectively.

04

4. Required and optional data

Required account, billing and assessment information is voluntarily provided but may be necessary to deliver the ordered service or examine an application. Optional profile fields and newsletter consent are not conditions for an assessment. Do not include irrelevant sensitive data or details about patients, clients or other third parties in supporting documents.

05

5. Individual document checks and recognition

Documents are submitted through secure account functions and kept in private file storage. The system may check file type, format, size, completeness, identifiers, versions and audit history; authorised administrators may categorise evidence and prepare it for individual review.

The Committee makes the final decision on the title, not an algorithm. If AI functions are introduced in the future to evaluate attachment contents or make decisions with significant effects, their scope, legal basis and individual rights must be disclosed before use. This Policy does not authorise unlimited AI analysis of submissions.

06

6. Cloudflare infrastructure

Cloudflare provides Workers hosting, the D1 database, private R2 file storage, network protection and essential technical logs. Original evidence is available only to appropriately authorised roles under server-side access controls. Processing may occur in different data centres and by infrastructure providers according to the service configuration and contracts.

We do not claim all information remains exclusively in the EU or that all technical operations take place in one country.

07

7. Brevo email delivery

Brevo is used for one-time sign-in codes, confirmations, case notifications and other transactional communications. For this purpose it may receive email addresses, recipient names, message identifiers, delivery status and minimal message content. Source evidence and private case files should remain in the secure Platform rather than ordinary email.

Where optional marketing is offered, it requires an appropriate separate legal basis and unsubscribe options. Provider information: https://www.brevo.com/legal/privacypolicy/.

08

8. Stripe payments and billing

Stripe may process identity, contact, payment and technical details needed for payment processing, refunds, fraud prevention and any subscriptions. The Operator and Platform normally receive transaction IDs, amount, currency, status and order-related information rather than complete card numbers supplied directly to Stripe.

Depending on the product and processing purpose, Stripe may be a payment processor, data processor, independent controller or — only if confirmed by the particular checkout — Merchant of Record. Provider information: https://stripe.com/privacy and https://stripe.com/au/legal/privacy-center.

09

9. International operations and data protection

The PsychCon platform is operated through cooperation between the Psychology Counselling Association and a business based in Australia that provides technical and administrative support and digital services.

As part of this cooperation, personal data may be processed both within and outside the European Economic Area, to the extent necessary for the proper operation of the platform.

Access to personal data is restricted to authorised persons, and processing is carried out using appropriate security measures and in accordance with applicable data protection laws.

Further information about the processing and protection of personal data is available at office@psychcon.org.

10

10. Data recipients and authorised persons

Depending on the operation, recipients may include Committee members, authorised TDP administrators, the Australian Operator and authorised personnel, Cloudflare, Brevo, Stripe, accounting and legal service providers and authorities entitled by law. Access is limited to what is necessary; not every vendor receives every user's data.

We do not sell applicant files as marketing databases. Applicable processor relationships should be governed by appropriate contractual arrangements.

11

11. Public certificate verification and published opinions

Once a decision has been published, unique QR codes or links may display the holder's name, level, reference, revision, issue date, issuer, current status and the Committee's opinion designated for publication. Anyone who possesses the unique code may access that information without an account.

Public code verification does not reveal private attachments, correspondence or full files. Sharing a PDF or QR code allows recipients to open the verification page; third-party copies or indexing may not be removable immediately.

12

12. Cookies, sessions and protection

The Platform uses essential session and security mechanisms including authentication cookies, CSRF checks, Turnstile and login rate limiting. Necessary server logs may be generated to prevent abuse and investigate incidents.

Optional analytics or marketing cookies requiring consent must not be activated before valid consent. Browser controls can disable cookies but turning off essential cookies may prevent sign-in.

13

13. Retention, archives and deletion

Account and application data is kept during the case and for any necessary appeal, evidence of correct decision-making and legal claims. Tax and accounting documents are kept as required by law. Certificate data may be needed while the authenticity register operates; logs are held as necessary for security; optional marketing data is kept until consent is withdrawn or another basis ends.

Different categories have different retention needs. After the applicable period, data is erased or anonymised subject to backup cycles and legal obligations. Closing an account does not automatically erase assessment records or historically issued certificates. More specific retention information can be requested where relevant.

14

14. Individual rights

Subject to applicable law, people may request access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability, object to processing, and withdraw consent at any time where consent is the legal ground. Applicable protections for solely automated decisions are also available where relevant.

Send requests to office@psychcon.org. Limited identity verification may be required. A complaint may be made to the President of the Polish Personal Data Protection Office (UODO) or another competent privacy regulator.

15

15. Security and incidents

Protective measures include role-based permissions, encrypted connections, private files, operation validation, audit records and access restrictions. No technical system is risk-free; incidents are investigated and notifications made where required by law.

16

16. Updates and contact

Policy version 1.0 takes effect on 17 November 2026. Material processing changes will be notified as required by law. Publishing an updated policy does not replace a required consent or other lawful basis.

Contact: office@psychcon.org. This Policy is available for printing and saving.
