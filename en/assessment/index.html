Explore every pathway to recognition

From early placements to established professional and academic achievements. The commission considers the course content, actual practice, professional training and supporting evidence — not just a degree title. All pathways below are proposed and remain subject to approval.

What may count?

Your complete learning and practice history matters.

Examples of relevant fields and specialisations include psychopedagogy, social rehabilitation and sociotherapy, educational therapy, special education, education and support for people with intellectual disabilities, education and therapy for autistic people, early childhood intervention, family counselling, educational counselling and welfare-focused pedagogy. These examples illustrate possible academic backgrounds; each applicant's actual studies and achievements are considered individually.

Studied abroad?

Degrees in Psychology, Counselling, Counselling Psychology, Psychology with Counselling and related fields may be considered. The commission considers the degree level, course content, placements and achievements to understand each person's professional record. The name of a degree alone does not determine whether the association will recognise that record.

Sociology, philosophy, education or other social sciences?

These backgrounds may be considered case by case when supported by relevant psychological or psychosocial skills. Additional learning and supervised practical experience may be required where the original degree did not cover essential psychological foundations.

Training and practical experience count, too

Provide evidence of further education, postgraduate training, supervision, volunteering and practice, for example in helping communication, developmental psychology, ethics, crisis support, trauma-informed practice or foundations of psychopathology. The commission may request a syllabus, contact hours, placement evidence or supplementary education. This does not require buying a course from any particular provider. Training does not replace degree-level requirements or the proposed publication thresholds for Levels III–IV.

The Psychological Counsellor TDP title recognises an individual's documented achievements within the association. It does not grant statutory professional rights or permission to practise a regulated profession.

00First steps into practice

Psychological Counsellor in Training

For psychology students who have completed at least two years of study, started practical training and engaged in volunteering or documented publication.

What the commission considers
  • Currently studying psychology, with at least two years completed.
  • Psychology-related placement started or completed.
  • Documented volunteering in psychology, education or psychosocial support, or an authored/co-authored publication with a DOI.

Examples of supporting evidence

  • Confirmation of psychology enrolment and completed year
  • Placement or internship evidence
  • Volunteering confirmation or DOI publication

An educational-stage recognition, not confirmation of graduation or permission to practise independently as a psychologist.

01A documented foundation

Psychological Counsellor TDP Level I

For graduates in psychology, education or related disciplines with at least a bachelor's degree, relevant placements and early documented practice.

What the commission considers
  • At least a bachelor's degree in psychology or a related field such as pedagogy, psychopedagogy, social rehabilitation, educational therapy, special education or social/human sciences with appropriate psychological preparation. The curriculum, not only the title, determines relevance.
  • Completed placement related to the course of study.
  • Professional or practical activity started or developing.
  • Documented volunteering, internship or DOI publication.

Examples of supporting evidence

  • Degree certificate and, where relevant, a transcript or course syllabus
  • Completed placement confirmation
  • Evidence of current activity
  • Volunteering, internship or DOI publication

Education and achievements from related disciplines and overseas programmes are reviewed individually. The commission may request evidence of supplementary training or gaps to be addressed; Level I does not provide legal permission to practise as a psychologist.

02Master’s education and practice

Psychological Counsellor TDP Level II

For graduates with a master's degree in psychology, pedagogy or selected related disciplines, relevant practice and further documented competencies.

What the commission considers
  • Master's degree in psychology, pedagogy (including psychopedagogy, social rehabilitation, sociotherapy, educational therapy or special education) or a related social/humanities discipline with appropriate psychological preparation. The commission assesses the curriculum and any bridging education individually.
  • Completed placement relevant to the field of study.
  • Professional activity in psychology started or continuing.
  • Documented volunteering, internship or DOI publication.

Examples of supporting evidence

  • Master's diploma, transcript or syllabus, including studies completed abroad
  • Placement confirmation
  • Employment or practice documentation
  • Volunteering, internship or DOI publication

Counselling and other relevant degrees, including those obtained abroad, may be considered on their curriculum and placements. Further training supports competence but does not replace a master's-level requirement.

03Established work and publications

Psychological Counsellor TDP Level III

For master's graduates in psychology, pedagogy or relevant related disciplines with at least three years of practice and five distinct DOI publications.

What the commission considers
  • Master's degree in psychology, pedagogy or another related field, with documented psychological preparation considered individually by the commission.
  • At least three years of documented work in psychology-related roles, counselling, psychosocial support or therapeutic education, in roles consistent with the applicant's education and experience.
  • At least five distinct scientific or specialist publications, each with its own DOI, authored or co-authored by the applicant.

Examples of supporting evidence

  • Master's diploma, curriculum evidence and records of relevant professional learning
  • Confirmation of at least three years of relevant work
  • Five distinct DOI publications and evidence of authorship

Widening the degree pathways remains a proposal. Courses and practice may strengthen the record but do not replace the current proposed requirement for five distinct DOI publications.

III
04Advanced professional and scientific record

Psychological Counsellor TDP Level IV

For master's graduates in psychology, pedagogy or relevant related fields with over five years of practice, scientific contribution and advanced professional competencies.

What the commission considers
  • A master's degree or higher in psychology, pedagogy or a related field, with appropriate psychological and practical preparation reviewed individually.
  • More than five years of documented professional work in psychology-related roles, counselling, psychosocial support or therapeutic education, in roles consistent with the applicant's education and experience.
  • At least five separate scientific or specialist publications with individual DOIs, authored or co-authored by the applicant.

Examples of supporting evidence

  • Master's degree or higher, course content and professional development evidence
  • Evidence of more than five years of professional work
  • Five distinct DOI publications and evidence of authorship

The commission considers continuity of practice, responsibilities and contribution to knowledge. Training adds to the record but does not replace five distinct DOI publications.

HSeparate from Levels I–IV

Honorary distinction

A special recognition of exceptional contributions to the association, education, scientific activity, the psychology community or social initiatives.

What the commission considers
  • Significant contribution to the association or its statutory aims.
  • Contribution to institutions, educational projects, scientific programmes or professional collaboration.
  • Individual, reasoned decision of the competent association body.

Examples of supporting evidence

  • Documented account of contributions
  • Confirmation of significant professional or social achievements
  • Materials illustrating contribution to the community

Not a Level V and not automatically subject to the numerical criteria for Levels I–IV.

Your review journey

From application to certificate.

Ten steps — from selecting a level and the first payment to the commission’s decision, the certificate and future progression.

  1. 01

    Before you begin

    Read the assessment principles

    Review the criteria for each level, required documents, terms and fees. Select the level that best matches your education, qualifications and practical experience.

  2. 02

    Passwordless sign-in

    Open the application portal

    Your email address is your account login and identifier. Each time you sign in, a new one-time code is sent to that address instead of using a traditional password.

  3. 03

    New application

    Select “New application”

    Once signed in, start a new application and choose the level you wish to request. That level is not guaranteed; the commission considers your actual professional record.

  4. 04

    First payment · PLN 300

    Accept the terms and pay for the review

    Read and accept the terms, payment conditions and required declarations, then select “Pay”. The one-off PLN 300 fee unlocks the application form and starts the process of preparing your submission for review. A second payment — PLN 150 per year for an active certificate — is due only after a positive decision.

  5. 05

    Form and supporting evidence

    Describe your background and upload documents

    Provide details of your schools and universities, internships, placements, employment, professional training and any publications. Upload supporting documents. If you have no publications, indicate this in the form; you can ask the commission for help arranging your first formal publication. This does not waive the publication requirements for Levels III–IV. Include all higher education, including overseas study; the commission considers curricula and psychological preparation individually. Attach files to each education, placement or work entry: up to 2 files in PDF, JPG, PNG or WebP, each up to 5 MB. Do not submit client data. Once complete, submit the application for review.

  6. 06

    Document checks

    Respond to the commission

    The commission checks the submitted documents and may request additional evidence, corrections, explanations or confirmation of particular experience. Respond to its requests through the portal. Review of a complete application usually takes 14–30 days from submission. The timeframe may be extended if additional documents, corrections or clarifications are required.

  7. 07

    Individual decision

    Receive the complete assessment outcome

    After examining your complete record, the commission issues a reasoned decision. It may grant the requested level, a lower or higher level, or decline to award a title.

  8. 08

    Second payment · PLN 150/year

    Activate your annual subscription

    If the decision is positive, pay the PLN 150 annual subscription. This provides access to an active certificate and its online verification. The subscription is not charged during the initial review.

  9. 09

    Polish and English documents

    Download your QR-coded certificate

    After paying the subscription, you can download two separate PDFs — Polish and English — with a QR code and full reasons for the decision. The certificate’s authenticity and current status can be verified online. View sample documents.

  10. 10

    Further options

    Appeal or apply for a higher level

    You may appeal a decision through the portal within 30 days of receiving it (when it is made available in your account). An appeal is assessed for a separate fee under the current price list and Terms, unless an authorised fee waiver applies. After the deadline, the appeal form is hidden. You may separately apply for a higher level after gaining further experience, qualifications or achievements; reassessment is also a paid service.

How does the commission review DOI publications?

A DOI identifies a publication. The commission verifies existence, authorship or co-authorship, subject matter and actual contribution. A DOI alone does not prove peer review or professional competence.

Corrections, appeals and reassessments

If a document requires changes, the commission identifies what needs to be corrected. An appeal challenges an existing decision; a reassessment starts a new case. Deadlines and procedures require approval.

A review fee, not a guaranteed outcome

Initial assessment, reassessment, appeal review and annual digital-profile access are separate services. View current pricing. Assessment payment does not guarantee approval.
